The Importance of the Working Stamp

The working stamp is the heart of every Nanoimprint Lithography (NIL) process. Every feature, sidewall angle, surface roughness and defect present on the stamp is faithfully transferred into the resist and ultimately into the final device. The quality of the stamp directly determines process yield, dimensional accuracy, pattern fidelity and manufacturing costs. Selecting the appropriate stamp material, fabrication strategy and surface treatment is therefore essential for successful NIL production.

Applications
  • Semiconductor manufacturing
  • Photonics and optical components
  • Microfluidics
  • Biotechnology
  • Sensors
  • Functional surfaces

Master Fabrication

High-quality masters are generally fabricated using high-resolution nanofabrication techniques such as Electron Beam Lithography (EBL), Laser Interference Lithography or Deep UV Lithography. Because these masters are expensive and time consuming to produce, they are usually preserved and used only for replication of multiple working stamps.

  • Highest dimensional accuracy
  • Nanometer resolution
  • Long-term reproducibility
  • Protection of valuable master structures

Hard Stamps

Hard stamps are typically manufactured from Silicon, Quartz, Nickel or Glass and provide the highest dimensional accuracy for UV and Thermal Nanoimprint Lithography.

Advantages

  • Excellent dimensional stability
  • Sub-10 nm resolution
  • Outstanding pattern fidelity
  • Excellent critical dimension control
  • Long lifetime

Challenges

  • Brittle materials
  • Particle sensitivity
  • High demolding forces
  • Difficult conformal contact on warped wafers
  • Higher manufacturing cost

Soft Stamps (PDMS)

Soft stamps based on PDMS provide excellent conformal contact and enable imprinting on curved, flexible and non-planar substrates while reducing imprint pressure.

Advantages

  • Excellent flexibility
  • Low imprint pressure
  • Large area patterning
  • Low fabrication cost
  • Simple replication

Challenges

  • Pattern deformation
  • Feature collapse
  • Limited mechanical stiffness
  • Swelling in organic solvents
  • Reduced lifetime
  • Resolution limitations for dense nanostructures

Hybrid Working Stamps

Modern NIL increasingly employs hybrid materials such as OrmoStamp®, OrmoComp®, PFPE and composite PDMS structures that combine the flexibility of soft materials with the dimensional stability of hard stamps. These materials provide improved mechanical properties, reduced shrinkage, better chemical resistance and significantly longer lifetime.

Critical Design Parameters

  • Feature size
  • Pitch
  • Aspect ratio
  • Feature depth
  • Sidewall angle
  • Corner radius
  • Surface roughness
  • Residual layer control
  • Anti-sticking coating quality

Common Challenges

  • Particle contamination
  • Stamp wear
  • Incomplete resist filling
  • Air trapping
  • Feature collapse
  • High demolding forces
  • Pattern distortion
  • Defect replication
  • Loss of anti-sticking layer
