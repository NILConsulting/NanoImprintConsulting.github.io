Why Resist Optimization Matters

The resist is one of the most critical elements of a successful Nanoimprint Lithography (NIL) process. Even with an excellent working stamp and a state-of-the-art imprint tool, inadequate resist properties can result in incomplete cavity filling, poor pattern fidelity, difficult demolding, excessive residual layers and reduced process yield. Optimizing the resist formulation is therefore essential for achieving reliable, repeatable and high-resolution nano-patterning.

Typical applications

  • Photonic structures
  • Micro-optics
  • Microfluidics
  • Biochips

UV Nanoimprint Resists

Most commercial UV Nanoimprint materials are based on two main polymer chemistries:

  • (Meth)acrylate-based resists offering very fast UV curing, low viscosity and excellent process throughput.
  • Epoxy-based resists providing lower polymerization shrinkage, superior mechanical stability and excellent chemical resistance.

Each chemistry presents distinct advantages and limitations. Selecting the appropriate material depends on the target application, feature size, aspect ratio, substrate, curing conditions and subsequent processing steps.

Viscosity Optimization

Viscosity is one of the most important parameters controlling resist filling during Nanoimprint Lithography. A resist with excessively high viscosity may not completely fill nanoscale cavities, leading to missing structures, trapped air bubbles and non-uniform residual layers. Conversely, an excessively low viscosity can cause overfilling, resist spreading, edge bead formation and difficulties controlling residual layer thickness. Achieving the correct viscosity is therefore essential for balancing rapid cavity filling with dimensional control and process stability.

UV Curing Optimization

UV curing determines the final crosslinked polymer network and directly influences pattern fidelity and demolding performance. Insufficient curing may leave the resist mechanically weak, causing deformation during demolding or subsequent processing. Excessive curing can increase internal stresses, polymerization shrinkage and adhesion to the stamp, resulting in higher demolding forces and shorter stamp lifetime. Optimizing UV dose, exposure time and photoinitiator concentration is therefore essential for obtaining robust, reproducible imprint results.

Mechanical Properties

The mechanical behaviour of the cured resist plays a major role in successful Nanoimprint Lithography. Depending on the application, the material may require:

  • High stiffness for dimensional stability
  • Controlled flexibility to reduce stress during demolding
  • Low shrinkage
  • Good adhesion to the substrate
  • Low adhesion to the working stamp
  • Excellent thermal and chemical stability

Balancing these properties is often challenging because improving one characteristic may negatively affect another. Material formulation therefore requires a comprehensive understanding of polymer chemistry and process integration.
